## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology role): the planned study design, data handling, measures, sample sizes, controls and reproducibility provisions must be strong enough that executing the plan would clear normal peer-review standards. From a cross-disciplinary angle, I read this as asking whether an outside evaluator could audit and rerun the planned measurements, but I do not score it.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain role): claims and descriptions of prior work must match the current state of the field, with correct terminology and correctly reported results. Adjacent-field readers depend on this accuracy to trust any transfer of ideas, but scoring belongs to the domain reviewer.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with methodology also eligible): the central thesis must hang together, the evidence planned must actually bear on the claims, and no reasoning flaw may undermine the core argument. I note that cross-field analogies can hide such flaws, but I leave the scoring to the eligible roles.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by me): the framing, key definitions and stated implications must be understandable to readers from neighbouring fields, and any claim that reaches into another discipline must be backed rather than asserted. This is the dimension I plan to score.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role): the organisation, clarity, figures and tables, and conformity with venue conventions are assessed here, including whether the planned output would fit the venue's format. Clear exposition also helps adjacent-field readers, but I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role): the work must suit the configured venue and offer an original, significant contribution for that readership. Cross-field significance can inform this judgement, but the score belongs to the editor-in-chief.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core terms, evaluation concepts and claimed implications are defined so readers from adjacent fields (AI assurance, ML evaluation, software engineering, formal methods) can follow them; whether claims that draw on another discipline, such as independence of AI error sources, oversight value or assurance-case style arguments, are supported by cited evidence or a planned measurement rather than asserted; and whether the planned results would transfer beyond the immediate community.
what_triggers_block: A central interdisciplinary claim on which the contribution depends is unsupported by evidence or planned measurement, or is framed so that adjacent-field readers would draw a materially wrong conclusion about what the planned results can show.
what_triggers_warn: Some key terms or cross-field implications are undefined, loosely used or overstated, or relevant adjacent-field work is missing, but the central contribution remains intelligible and its main claims stay supportable after targeted clarification.
