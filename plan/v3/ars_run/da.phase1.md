D2 (domain_accuracy): I read this as asking whether the claims about the field and about prior work would hold up if an expert went looking for misrepresentation, outdated evidence or misused terminology. A challenger checks whether prior work is set up as a weaker straw position than it really is. This dimension belongs to the domain seat and I will not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This is my owned dimension. It asks whether the central thesis is internally consistent and whether the planned evidence could actually support the claims made, or whether the argument depends on fallacies, circular reasoning, unexamined premises or a gap between what the design can show and what the paper says it will show. Because the submission is a plan, I judge whether the planned argument, if executed, would earn its conclusions, including whether reasonable alternative explanations for the expected outcome are left open.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): I read this as asking whether readers from adjacent fields could follow the framing and whether any claims that cross disciplinary lines are backed rather than asserted. An adversary checks for jargon that hides weak links and for implications claimed beyond what the field boundary supports. The perspective seat scores this, not me.

D5 (writing_and_structure): I read this as asking whether the plan is organised and clear and whether the planned paper would fit venue conventions in length, form and anonymisation. A challenger checks whether vague structure conceals missing pieces of the argument. The editor seat scores this, not me.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): I read this as asking whether the planned paper suits the target venue and would make an original, significant contribution, as opposed to an incremental or overstated one. An adversary asks the "so what" question hardest here. The editor seat scores this, not me.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated thesis and each headline claim follow from the planned evidence without hidden premises, circularity, overgeneralisation, or unaddressed alternative explanations, and whether the claims stay consistent across sections of the plan.
what_triggers_block: The central claim cannot be supported by the planned evidence even if executed perfectly, or the argument relies on a fallacy or an untested premise that the conclusion depends on, so the plan needs substantive redesign of its argument before it could succeed.
what_triggers_warn: The core argument is sound but some secondary claims overreach the planned evidence, some alternative explanations are acknowledged only weakly, or minor inconsistencies between sections need tightening.
what_triggers_fatal: The thesis is self-contradictory or the plan's own design would, by construction, be unable to distinguish the claimed result from its most obvious rival explanation, so no execution of the plan could yield a coherent paper.
