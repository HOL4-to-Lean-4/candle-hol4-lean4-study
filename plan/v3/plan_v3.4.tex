\section{Goal and framing}\label{sec:goal}
The question is: \emph{in AI-made HOL4-to-Lean translations of two developments (eight translations), which fidelity errors do current model audits find, which do they miss, how often are their stated reasons right, and what is left after a check that needs no model?} The contribution is a measured audit of real translation errors, with a fidelity contract and a key of known errors. The word ``certification'' is not used: finding known errors does not show that none remain. Claims of formal correspondence need a theorem, and this study does not prove one.

\paragraph{Main claims, as far as the data allow.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which of the known errors each arm finds, and which kinds it misses. Reported with each arm's setting in full, with the scripts (S0) beside the models, and without the errors that are provisional.
\item Errors found in the audit that were not in the key, and which of these a script would also have found. These are lower bounds.
\item What a reviewer adds or removes.
\item How often the stated reason is right, by the author's check of a selected set of findings (Section~\ref{sec:check}).
\end{enumerate}
What is not claimed: that any model family is better; that the totals describe translation errors in general (the known errors are selected, unevenly spread over the translations, and some entered the key through the scripts); anything about false accusations as a rate, because clean modules are few and many of their findings are undecided by the contract.

The FormaliSE 2027 experience report (study~1) migrated CakeML's type-soundness closure, 269 theorems, with a Claude translator, a Gemini validator and a human expert who adjudicated every finding; proof attempts later exposed defects that this review missed. What this study adds over study~1:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a measured audit on real errors of eight translations, scored against a fidelity contract;
\item arms from two model families and reviewers from two, with the same brief, plus a script baseline;
\item typed HOL4 statements exported from the kernel and a full ledger of tokens, money and time;
\item a second development (Candle's \code{proves\_sound}), and a proving run whose proofs Lean checks.
\end{enumerate}
Transferable output: a taxonomy of HOL4-to-Lean semantic gaps with, for each, which setup found it, and a benchmark of known errors (key, units, checks).

Translation (``frontier models translate these closures with few errors of the kinds we checked, at \$7--31'') is stated only as an observation. Target: ITP 2027 regular paper (16 pages plus references, LIPIcs, lightweight double-blind, abstract 8 February, paper 15 February 2027 AoE). CICM 2027 (about 1 April) is the fallback. The ITP call requires AI use to be disclosed in the main text and does not allow substantial AI-generated text in the paper.

Candle translations already run: Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5 (with lessons and without lessons) and Sonnet 5.5, no V-P found in any by the earlier audits; see the re-scoring in Section~\ref{sec:rescore} for V-C.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Direct translation by Claude Fable 5.2} (21 Nov): all 278 kernel-closure statements, with \code{sorry} proofs. It took 40 minutes and cost about US\$31 at API prices. Audit: 0 defects, 0 suspects, 27 notes.
\item \textbf{Direct translation by Claude Opus 5.5} (22 Nov, same briefs): 45 minutes, about US\$19. Audit: 0 defects, 0 suspects, 22 notes. A comparison found no difference in meaning from Fable. In form, Fable follows the HOL4 text more literally in 8 places and Opus in 2.
\item \textbf{Proving by Fable 5.2} (21 Nov): 269 of 269 type-soundness theorems from the study-1 \code{sorry} benchmark, in 62 minutes, without help.
\item \textbf{Audit weaknesses seen.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Three same-family Claude agents in related sessions (two auditors, one comparison agent) gave the same wrong reason for a correct verdict. They said HOL4 fills the missing cases of \code{dest\_var} and similar constants with \code{ARB}; these constants are defined through datatype destructors and \code{Prim\_rec}, which leave those cases unspecified (checked in HOL4 on 22 November). HOL4's \code{Define} does complete missing patterns with \code{ARB}, so the auditors' mistake was applying \code{Define}'s rule to definitions it does not define. Two auditors plus one comparison agent is not ``independent'' evidence.
  \item A Gemini review found one type-signature difference in Fable's translation, which no meaning-level audit had reported.
  \item The audits ran in the same session as the project chat, and the harness passed two chat messages to every agent.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
These runs show two things. First, a strong single model now translates this closure with few or no meaning defects, so the value of any workflow has to be shown by what its checks catch, not by the raw translation. Second, the checks themselves need measuring, because same-family auditors can share mistakes.

\section{Fidelity contract (frozen before any scored run)}\label{sec:contract}
\paragraph{Terms.} HOL4 side: the 278 target statements and the definitions, inductive relations and datatypes they need, as exported by the kernel (Section~\ref{sec:hol4}), with constants and types written as \code{Theory\$name}. A \emph{map} $M$ sends each HOL4 type operator and constant to a Lean type or term; it is written in \code{CONVENTIONS.md}. $\Phi$ is the set of facts about the mapped constants derivable, under the HOL4 inference rules, from the named theorems that the pinned HOL4 theories \emph{save} and their dependencies (the saved interface; Astra's decision, delegated by the author, 24 November). It matters for some functions with incomplete patterns: for \code{termsem0}, HOL4 saves only the four-clause \code{termsem\_def}, so under this reading a fixed value for the other case is V-C. The primitive definition that TFL makes was not found in the saved theory; whether it fixes the case to \code{ARB} was not checked.
\paragraph{Violations.} A Lean declaration is
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \textbf{preservation violation} (V-P) if its Lean statement is not the $M$-image of the HOL4 statement up to provable equivalence, and the difference changes which theorem the Lean statement asserts: too strong, too weak, wrong quantifier or hypothesis, wrong operator, wrong type. A proper specialisation of a polymorphic HOL4 theorem (a type parameter replaced by a concrete type) is V-P when it removes instances of the claim;
\item a \textbf{conservativity violation} (V-C) if a Lean definition makes an equation or inequation about mapped constants provable that is not in $\Phi$. Fixing \code{HD []}, \code{dest\_var} of a non-variable, \code{RESULT (Clash t)}, \code{CHR n} for $n\ge 256$, \code{FUN\_FMAP} on an infinite set or \code{SEG} out of range to one value is V-C when that equation is not in $\Phi$, even though every target is unaffected; a shared \code{ARB} across unrelated types or constants, and \code{Classical.epsilon} of predicates that all hold, are checked by the same test;
\item a \textbf{type deviation} (note, not a violation) if the Lean type of an unused binder differs from HOL4's inferred type but the statements are provably equivalent;
\item a \textbf{faithful encoding} (note) if it uses another representation of HOL4 data (finite maps, words, sets, characters) with a stated map and proved equations, or adds a \code{[Nonempty]} or \code{[Inhabited]} hypothesis on a type variable that HOL4 types satisfy.
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Tier 2 (Astra, 24 November).} An equation whose proof needs a Hilbert-choice term is scored in a separate secondary category, only when Lean proves a specific equation in the mapped vocabulary that the saved interface leaves open; using \code{Classical.epsilon} alone is not a finding. Results are reported with a sensitivity analysis that adds verified tier-2 cases to the primary score. \paragraph{Decisions.} \code{HD []} and the like are V-C (decided by the author, 24 November). \code{ARB} and Hilbert choice must satisfy exactly the HOL4 equations of the specified constant. Unused binders and duplicate binder names: notes unless the statement's instances change. Finite maps: equal under a proved bijection, notes. Proof script style, names, argument order and alpha-renaming never count. A violation label needs a witness: a Lean or HOL4 check, or a proof. Labels are written and frozen before any scored run. The author's 24 November rule means the Opus, Sonnet and no-lessons baselines contain V-C instances; ``0 meaning defects'' in earlier results means 0 V-P found by the model audits. Section~\ref{sec:rescore} reports the re-scoring.
\paragraph{Operational form of V-C (draft).} Every total Lean definition proves some Lean-only equation, so the test is restricted to equations written with \emph{mapped} constants only (including the images of \code{ARB} and \code{@}). A V-C instance needs (a) a Lean proof of such an equation and (b) evidence that HOL4 does not prove it: a HOL4 proof of its negation is not expected; instead, two models of the HOL4 defining theorem that disagree on the equation. A failed HOL4 proof search is not evidence.
\paragraph{Re-scoring of the baselines.}\label{sec:rescore} The four baselines are scored against this contract with kernel checks where possible: for each constant fixed in \code{CONVENTIONS.md}, a Lean script tests whether a concrete equation not in $\Phi$ is provable. Counts: V-P (by hand and HOL4 query), V-C (by Lean script), type deviations, encodings.

\section{Workflow}
\emph{Status.} The loop below (validation rounds, review, grounding, repair, repeated) was planned in version~3.3 and was \emph{not} run in this form. What ran is: translation, the structural scans, one audit of every module per arm, one review pass, the repair of the Candle base for proving, and proving. The paper reports these; the loop is described so that the reader can see what was planned. The open question whether to run it is listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Freeze.} Pin the commits, the manifest, the briefs, the model identifiers and settings, and the budgets. Record their hashes in the repository before any model sees the target.

\item \textbf{Isolation.} Every model run starts in a fresh session on the PC that holds only its workspace. Claude runs use headless Claude Code (\code{claude -p}) in a separate directory, not a project-chat session, so that no chat message can reach an agent. Codex and \code{agy} runs get only their input files. A script scans every transcript for paths outside the workspace and for web or git use.

\item \textbf{Translation (round 0).} This repeats the baseline procedure: stage~1 (all definitions plus \code{CONVENTIONS.md}), then stage~2 (four agents writing the statements). The translators see the full HOL4 scripts, proofs included. The result must build, and a kernel check confirms that exactly the 278 targets use \code{sorry}.

\item \textbf{Structural scans} run on every revision:
\begin{itemize}
\item statements that are \code{True} or vacuous;
\item definitions without bodies, and uses of \code{partial}, \code{opaque}, \code{axiom}, \code{native\_decide} or \code{decreasing\_by sorry};
\item extra or missing typeclass hypotheses;
\item bound variables whose type differs from HOL4's inferred type;
\item byte identity of frozen statements.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Validation round $k$ (at most 3).} Astra audits the whole translation once, module by module, against the HOL4 scripts and library sources. Run-to-run variance is measured in the audit experiment, not here.

\item \textbf{Evidence rule.} Every finding must cite the Lean and HOL4 lines and say what would show the difference: a Lean counterexample, a HOL4 query, or a proof. A finding without that is marked unverified, but is still passed on.

\item \textbf{Review.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Neon receives each finding with the HOL4 and Lean pair and written instructions, the same material the human expert received in study~1.
\item It returns accept or reject with a reason. For each accepted finding it gives the class (defect, by design, misread convention, misquote) and the repair it proposes.
\item No finding changes the translation without an accept from Neon.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Grounding.}
\begin{itemize}
\item For every finding, accepted or rejected, whose claim can be checked mechanically, a script runs the check (Section~\ref{sec:hol4}).
\item If the check contradicts the finding, the finding goes back to Neon once, with the check output.
\item We record every case where the check overturns the reviewer.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Repair.} Claude repairs accepted defects with a minimal edit. Each repair is re-checked by the structural scans and by Astra on the changed declarations only.

\item \textbf{Stopping rule.} The loop stops in the first round in which no finding survives review and grounding, with at most 3 rounds.

\item \textbf{Proving}, one round (Section~\ref{sec:proving}).

\item \textbf{Back to review.} Every defect that a proof attempt exposes, such as a false statement, a missing hypothesis or an unprovable definition equation, goes to Astra and Neon, then to repair and the structural scans.

\item \textbf{Records.} Session logs, commits, findings, verdicts, check outputs, the cost ledger and a scripted counter, all packaged as an anonymous Zenodo artifact.
\end{enumerate}

\section{HOL4 as oracle}\label{sec:hol4}
HOL4 and the package are built on the PC (WSL Ubuntu, \code{scripts/build.sh native}; 10 theories, about 16 minutes, 24 November). Two tools live in \code{tools/hol4\_query/}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Typed kernel export.} Every target statement is printed as a raw kernel term (variables, constants as \code{Theory\$name}, application, abstraction, all types explicit), with hypotheses. 278 of 278 were exported on 24 November, 0 with hypotheses, all well typed. Its SHA-256 is recorded and will be pinned at the freeze. The export is for reference and scans only; translators and auditors do not receive it. The exported terms and the HOL4 type signatures of constants are the ground truth for the structural scans (S0).
\item \textbf{Query tool.} Four query kinds: print a theorem with types; parse a term and show what its constants resolve to; evaluate a ground term; try a short proof. A failed short proof is never a refutation. Every query is logged.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{What the oracle can decide.} Overload resolution and statement shapes (from the export), elaborated types, finite ground evaluations, and theorem identity: mechanical. Quantified, higher-order or inductive claims, anything through \code{Classical.epsilon}, and unprovability: not mechanical. For those, a witness must be a proof of the negation, a checked counterexample in a concrete instance, or a Lean proof of inequivalence. Otherwise the label is \emph{unresolved}.
\paragraph{Grounding.} For every finding, accepted or rejected, a script runs the cited check where the check is mechanical, using the query tool or \code{\#eval}/\code{decide} in Lean, with a 60 s limit. Checks that cannot run are recorded with the reason. Grounding scripts are separate from the interactive tool access that any auditor has, so that the two effects can be separated; scripted grounding was not run in the audit on real errors.

\section{Audit on real errors (main result)}\label{sec:audit}
\paragraph{Units.} One unit is one Lean module with the HOL4 sources it corresponds to and the other modules as context (all read-only, comments removed): 63 modules of 8 translations (four Candle, three type soundness, study~1). Some hold known errors and some hold none.
\paragraph{Key.} Known errors of three kinds: open values fixed (V-C), types too specific (V-P, in definitions and in statements), and the study-1 errors. They were known before the audit from conservativity tests, the statement scan, the type-variable count and study~1's failed proofs (\code{audit/real/key.json}; the one correction after the run is in \code{final.py}). The entries about statements are provisional until the author has scored the 51-row sheet. The key was made by Claude from these checks, not by the author. Counts per kind and translation: \code{audit/real/TABLES.md}.
\paragraph{Arms, as run.} Each audit run saw every unit once, same brief (\code{BRIEF\_audit.md}): S1 Astra; S3a and S3b two runs of Opus; S8 their union; S4 Astra's findings reviewed by Neon; S6 Opus run a's findings reviewed by Opus; S0 scripts (statement scan and type-variable count). Not run and not claimed: S2 (Neon alone), S5 and S6g and A1+G (scripted grounding of findings), S7. Astra ran once, Opus twice, so the interval for the comparison does not include Astra's variation.
\paragraph{Tool access, as run.} All arms worked in an isolated folder with the Lean module, its neighbours and the HOL4 sources, read-only, with file tools. None had the HOL4 query tool, which version~3.3 planned for every arm, and the brief told them not to run Lean or HOL4. Astra also used a JavaScript tool built into Codex (30 calls) to read and match files; one Opus call used Bash; a scan found no path outside the unit folder except tool-result files and no network call. The first 9 Neon reviews ran without the sentence restricting them to file tools.
\paragraph{Limits.} No token caps (the author set none for Opus and Sonnet). Queues and quotas delayed runs; the times are in the ledger.
\paragraph{Measures.} Known errors found per arm and per kind, per translation, with and without the provisional statements; S0 alone and S0 together with an arm, because part of the key comes from the scripts; findings outside the key, and which of them a script would also have flagged; review decisions; joint misses. The comparison of S1 with S3a and with S8 is a paired bootstrap over modules, with sign flips, and left one translation out (\code{audit/real/TABLES.md}, \code{report.py}). Matching is by script on the declaration name and, where needed, the class. Reasons are not judged by that script.
\paragraph{Findings outside the key.} Some were confirmed by Claude with Lean checks or HOL4 types (\code{audit/real/proofs}); the author's check decides about them in the paper. Findings on study-1 modules outside the key are not adjudicated and are counted neither way.
\paragraph{Pilot with planted defects.} Ten units (\code{audit/pilot/RESULTS.md}), reported as a pilot only. The mutant pool, the decoys and the witnesses stay in the repository and are not used further.

\section{Check of the reasons by the author}\label{sec:check}
\paragraph{Who.} The author, who knows HOL4 and Lean. The author designed the study and the contract, and is therefore not independent of it; the check is blinded to the run, the reviewer verdict, the translation and the result so far (the author stated on 25 November that they had not seen the results printed earlier in the work; the documents the author needs for the check hold no result). Claude selected the rows and wrote the sheet, and the author decides the answers without help from a model. A second reader on a sample is recommended and not arranged.
\paragraph{Order.} First the 51-row sheet (\code{baselines/ts\_runs\_v2/scoring}), then the 108-row sheet (\code{audit/real/check}).
\paragraph{Rows (fixed by script, seed \code{check-20261126}).} 108 rows, 91 declarations: findings of the three audit runs, chosen by rule, and some controls. The rule and the counts are in \code{audit/real/check/SPEC.md}, committed with the sheet and read by the author after answering.
\paragraph{Questions.} Is the difference real; which kind; is the stated reason right, partly, or wrong.
\paragraph{Analysis (fixed before the first answer, \code{analyse.py}).} Answers per stratum and arm; controls; known errors refuted by the check (removed from the key); recall per arm before and after; recall when the reason must also be right; the S1 against S8 difference with the module bootstrap; findings outside the key confirmed, refuted, or open. The 51-row sheet decides the provisional errors. If the control answers fail the threshold in \code{SPEC.md}, the check is reported as unreliable. Unresolved findings are counted neither way. Errors that the rule leaves out of the sheet are not checked one by one; a random sample gives an interval for them.

\section{Proving}\label{sec:proving}
Done (25 November), without HOL4 proofs, on the repaired base; 278 of 278 (Candle) and 267 of 267 (type soundness), Lean checked, statements and definitions unchanged (\code{proving\_nohol4/RESULTS.md}). The role is the one planned: one run, as one-sided evidence: proofs that fail or a statement that a proof refutes expose preservation violations that are too strong; a proof of every statement shows nothing about weak or wrong statements. Claude Opus 5.5 proves the \code{sorry} statements (not on mutants); definitions and statements are frozen by hash; only lemmas may be added. Measures: statements proved, transitive axiom audit, unchanged statements and definitions, statements refuted. A failed search is reported as \emph{unproved}; a statement counts as \emph{false} only with a Lean proof of its negation or a checked counterexample. Budget one run with a wall-clock cap and an output-token cap. A second proof round and a second prover family are not part of the paper. Earlier runs in which the prover saw HOL4 proofs broke the plan's rule; they are reported as such and not as the result.

\section{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.]

\item How often is the stated reason right, for the findings the author checks, and what does the check change in the key?

\section{Threats to validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Contract choice.} Conservativity counts choices that HOL4 leaves open as violations. A different contract gives different labels; both label sets (V-P, V-C) are reported separately.
\item \textbf{Known errors} are selected: part came from scripts, the rest from named-constant tests and proofs, and they are unevenly spread over the translations. Errors outside the key show the key is incomplete; general discovery is not measured.
\item \textbf{One run of Astra, two of Opus}, so run-to-run variation of Astra is unknown, and modules of one translation are not independent; intervals are by module and conditional on the key.
\item \textbf{The check} is by the author, who designed the study, against claims that the author sees; the reason for each control being right or wrong rests on the scripts. Claude selected the rows. Reading the claim first can anchor the answer.
\item \textbf{Exploratory} (see the first page): the analysis of known errors was chosen after the runs; only the check is fixed in advance.
\item \textbf{Model families.} Results hold for these configurations; no general claim about families. Agreement among agents of one family is not independent evidence.
\item \textbf{Oracle limits} (Section~\ref{sec:hol4}): unresolved findings are reported as such.
\item \textbf{Human effort} is logged (date, task, minutes, expertise needed). The workflow has no human expert in the loop, but the author checks and scores; Claude drafts briefs, labels, checks and this plan for the author's approval.
\item \textbf{Proof visibility.} Translators and auditors see the HOL4 proofs; provers do not. Contamination: CakeML and Candle are public, so all models may have seen them; a small renaming probe is optional.
\item \textbf{Model drift, CLI versions}: pinned and recorded.
\item \textbf{One target closure} in the audit, in one source logic, plus the type-soundness closure for real defects.
\end{itemize}
