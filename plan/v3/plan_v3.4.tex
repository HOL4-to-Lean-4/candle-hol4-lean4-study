\section{Goal and framing}\label{sec:goal}
The question is: \emph{in AI-made HOL4-to-Lean translations of two developments (eight translations), which fidelity errors do current model audits find, which do they miss, how often are their stated reasons right, and what is left after a check that needs no model?} The contribution is a measured audit of real translation errors, with a fidelity contract and a key of known errors. The word ``certification'' is not used: finding known errors does not show that none remain.

\paragraph{Main claims.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which of the known errors each arm finds, and which kinds it misses, with each arm's setting in full and the scripts (S0) beside the models, with and without the errors that were provisional.
\item Errors found in the audit that were not in the key, and which of these a script would also have found.
\item What a reviewer adds or removes.
\item How often the stated reason is right, by a human check of a selected set of findings (Section~\ref{sec:check}).
\end{enumerate}

The FormaliSE 2027 experience report (study~1) migrated CakeML's type-soundness closure with a Claude translator, a Gemini validator and a human expert who adjudicated every finding; proof attempts later exposed defects that this review missed. Study~1 counts 269 theorems and this study 267 for the same closure (Section~\ref{sec:counts}). What this study adds over study~1:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a measured audit on real errors of eight translations, scored against a fidelity contract;
\item arms from two model families and reviewers from two, with the same brief, plus a script baseline;
\item typed HOL4 statements exported from the kernel and a full ledger of tokens, money and time;
\item a second development (Candle's \code{proves\_sound}), and a proving run whose proofs Lean checks.
\end{enumerate}
Transferable output: a taxonomy of HOL4-to-Lean semantic gaps with, for each, which setup found it, and a benchmark of known errors (key, units, checks).

Translation (``frontier models translate these closures with few errors of the kinds we checked, at \$7--31'') is stated only as an observation. Target: ITP 2027 regular paper (16 pages plus references, LIPIcs, lightweight double-blind, abstract 8 February, paper 15 February 2027 AoE). CICM 2027 (about 1 April) is the fallback. The ITP call requires AI use to be disclosed in the main text and does not allow substantial AI-generated text in the paper.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Direct translation by Claude Fable 5.2} (21 Nov): all 278 kernel-closure statements, with \code{sorry} proofs. It took 40 minutes and cost about US\$31 at API prices. Audit: 0 defects, 0 suspects, 27 notes.
\item \textbf{Direct translation by Claude Opus 5.5} (22 Nov, same briefs): 45 minutes, about US\$19. Audit: 0 defects, 0 suspects, 22 notes. A comparison found no difference in meaning from Fable. In form, Fable follows the HOL4 text more literally in 8 places and Opus in 2.
\item \textbf{Proving by Fable 5.2} (21 Nov): 269 of 269 theorems of the study-1 \code{sorry} benchmark (study~1's count, Section~\ref{sec:counts}), in 62 minutes, without help.
\item \textbf{Audit weaknesses seen.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Three same-family Claude agents in related sessions (two auditors, one comparison agent) gave the same wrong reason for a correct verdict. They said HOL4 fills the missing cases of \code{dest\_var} and similar constants with \code{ARB}; these constants are defined through datatype destructors and \code{Prim\_rec}, which leave those cases unspecified (checked in HOL4 on 22 November). HOL4's \code{Define} does complete missing patterns with \code{ARB}, so the auditors' mistake was applying \code{Define}'s rule to definitions it does not define.
  \item A Gemini review found one type-signature difference in Fable's translation, which no meaning-level audit had reported.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
These runs show two things. First, a strong single model now translates this closure with few or no meaning defects, so the value of any workflow has to be shown by what its checks catch, not by the raw translation. Second, the checks themselves need measuring, because same-family auditors can share mistakes.

\section{Fidelity contract}\label{sec:contract}
\paragraph{Terms.} HOL4 side: the target statements and the definitions, inductive relations and datatypes they need, as exported by the kernel (Section~\ref{sec:hol4}), with constants and types written as \code{Theory\$name}. A \emph{map} $M$ sends each HOL4 type operator and constant to a Lean type or term; it is written in \code{CONVENTIONS.md}. $\Phi$ is the set of facts about the mapped constants derivable, under the HOL4 inference rules, from the named theorems that the pinned HOL4 theories \emph{save} and their dependencies (the saved interface; Astra's decision, delegated by the author, 24 November). Kernel definitions that a theory does not save under a name are not part of it (author, 27 November). It matters for functions with incomplete patterns: for \code{termsem0}, HOL4 saves only the four-clause \code{termsem\_def}, so under this reading a fixed value for the other case, \code{ARB} included, is V-C, although TFL's kernel definition gives \code{ARB} there.
\paragraph{Violations.} A Lean declaration is
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \textbf{preservation violation} (V-P) if its Lean statement is not the $M$-image of the HOL4 statement up to provable equivalence, and the difference changes which theorem the Lean statement asserts: too strong, too weak, wrong quantifier or hypothesis, wrong operator, wrong type. A proper specialisation of a polymorphic HOL4 theorem (a type parameter replaced by a concrete type) is V-P when it removes instances of the claim;
\item a \textbf{conservativity violation} (V-C) if a Lean definition makes an equation or inequation about mapped constants provable that is not in $\Phi$, established by the test below. Fixing \code{HD []}, \code{dest\_var} of a non-variable, \code{RESULT (Clash t)}, \code{CHR n} for $n\ge 256$, \code{FUN\_FMAP} on an infinite set or \code{SEG} out of range to one value is V-C when that equation is not in $\Phi$, even though every target is unaffected; a shared \code{ARB} across unrelated types or constants, and \code{Classical.epsilon} of predicates that all hold, are checked by the same test;
\item a \textbf{type deviation} (note, not a violation) if the Lean type of an unused binder differs from HOL4's inferred type but the statements are provably equivalent;
\item a \textbf{faithful encoding} (note) if it uses another representation of HOL4 data (finite maps, words, sets, characters) with a stated map and proved equations, or adds a \code{[Nonempty]} or \code{[Inhabited]} hypothesis on a type variable that HOL4 types satisfy.
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Tier 2 (Astra, 24 November).} An equation whose proof needs a Hilbert-choice term is scored in a separate secondary category, only when Lean proves a specific equation in the mapped vocabulary that the saved interface leaves open; using \code{Classical.epsilon} alone is not a finding. Results are reported with a sensitivity analysis that adds verified tier-2 cases to the primary score.
\paragraph{Decisions.} \code{HD []} and the like are V-C (decided by the author, 24 November). \code{ARB} and Hilbert choice must satisfy exactly the HOL4 equations of the specified constant. Unused binders and duplicate binder names: notes unless the statement's instances change. Finite maps: equal under a proved bijection, notes. Proof script style, names, argument order and alpha-renaming never count. A violation label needs a witness: a Lean or HOL4 check, or a proof. The author's 24 November rule means the Opus, Sonnet and no-lessons baselines contain V-C instances; ``0 meaning defects'' in earlier results means 0 V-P found by the model audits.
\paragraph{Test for V-C.} Every total Lean definition proves some Lean-only equation, so the test is restricted to equations $e$ written with \emph{mapped} constants only (including the images of \code{ARB} and \code{@}). A V-C label needs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item a Lean proof of $e$;
\item a counterexample: an interpretation in which every saved theorem holds and $e$ is false, obtained by changing the value of one constant (or of \code{ARB}) on a region $P$;
\item the kernel introduction of the changed constant, read from the HOL4 and CakeML sources: a specification (including primitive recursion, which leaves missing cases open), a constant definition, or a TFL definition, whose kernel definition fills missing patterns with \code{ARB};
\item for a specification: a HOL4 proof that the changed value satisfies it for every choice on $P$ (\code{ARB} has no axiom); for a TFL definition, whose \code{ARB} completion is not saved: a replacement defined by the saved clauses and an arbitrary catch-all, and a HOL4 proof of every saved theorem that mentions the constant for it.
\end{enumerate}
For a specification, (c) and (d) make the new interpretation a model of the HOL4 theory, with the constants defined from the changed one reinterpreted by their own definitions, so every saved theorem holds in it (soundness of HOL with loose specifications). For a TFL definition, only the constant changes and every saved theorem that mentions it is proved, so all saved theorems hold. Either way, two interpretations that satisfy one defining theorem but disagree with another saved theorem are excluded. A HOL4 proof of the negation of $e$ is not expected, and a failed HOL4 proof search is not evidence. Where (c) or (d) is missing, the label stays \emph{unsure}: it is not counted as V-C, and in the tables it counts as unresolved. The same rule holds for tier-2 cases and for V-C findings outside the key. As a supplement, a script rewrites every saved theorem that mentions the changed constant and tries fixed HOL4 tactics; its failures are not refutations. The V-C entries of the key were first made with the defining theorem only; the recheck with this test (27 November, \code{audit/real/vc\_recheck/RECHECK.md}) confirms all 63 pairs. For \code{termsem0}, the constants defined from it (\code{satisfies0}, \code{models0}, \code{entails0}) are redefined from the replacement, and all 25 saved theorems that mention any of the four are proved for it in HOL4.
\paragraph{Re-scoring of the baselines.}\label{sec:rescore} The four baselines are scored against this contract with kernel checks where possible: for each constant fixed in \code{CONVENTIONS.md}, a Lean script tests whether a concrete equation not in $\Phi$ is provable, and the test above decides the label. Counts: V-P (by hand and HOL4 query), V-C, type deviations, encodings.

\section{HOL4 as oracle}\label{sec:hol4}
HOL4 and the package are built on the PC (WSL Ubuntu, \code{scripts/build.sh native}; 10 theories, about 16 minutes, 24 November). Two tools live in \code{tools/hol4\_query/}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Typed kernel export.} Every target statement is printed as a raw kernel term (variables, constants as \code{Theory\$name}, application, abstraction, all types explicit), with hypotheses. 278 of 278 Candle statements were exported on 24 November, 0 with hypotheses, all well typed. The export is for reference and scans only; translators and auditors do not receive it. The exported terms and the HOL4 type signatures of constants are the ground truth for the structural scans (S0).
\item \textbf{Query tool.} Four query kinds: print a theorem with types; parse a term and show what its constants resolve to; evaluate a ground term; try a short proof. A fifth, needed by the V-C test, lists the saved theorems that mention given constants. A failed short proof is never a refutation. Every query is logged.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{What the oracle can decide.} Overload resolution and statement shapes (from the export), elaborated types, finite ground evaluations, theorem identity, and the list of saved theorems about a constant: mechanical. Quantified, higher-order or inductive claims, anything through \code{Classical.epsilon}, and unprovability: not mechanical. For those, a witness must be a proof of the negation, a checked counterexample in a concrete instance, or a Lean proof of inequivalence. Otherwise the label is \emph{unresolved}.

\section{Audit on real errors (main result)}\label{sec:audit}
\paragraph{Units.} One unit is one Lean module with the HOL4 sources it corresponds to and the other modules as context (read-only, comments removed): 63 modules of 8 translations (four Candle, three type soundness, study~1). Some hold known errors and some hold none.
\paragraph{Key.} Known errors of three kinds: open values fixed (V-C), types too specific (V-P, in definitions and in statements), and the study-1 errors. They were known before the audit from conservativity tests, the statement scan, the type-variable count and study~1's failed proofs (\code{audit/real/key.json}; the one correction after the run is in \code{final.py}). The entries about statements were provisional until the author scored the 51-row sheet. Counts per kind and translation: \code{audit/real/TABLES.md}.
\paragraph{Arms.} Each audit run saw every unit once, with the same brief (\code{BRIEF\_audit.md}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=S\arabic*.]\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item scripts: statement scan and type-variable count, against the typed export (no model);
\item Astra;
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item Opus (two runs, S3a and S3b);
\item Astra's findings reviewed by Neon (accept or reject);
\setcounter{enumi}{5}
\item Opus run a's findings reviewed by a separate Opus session;
\setcounter{enumi}{7}
\item union of two Opus runs (S3a, S3b).
\end{enumerate}
Arms reuse the audits: S4 uses S1, S6 uses S3a, S8 uses S3a and S3b.
\paragraph{Tool access.} Every auditor worked in an isolated folder with the Lean module, its neighbours and the HOL4 sources, read-only, with file tools, and was told not to run Lean or HOL4.
\paragraph{Measures.} Known errors found per arm and per kind, per translation, with and without the provisional statements; S0 alone and S0 together with an arm; findings outside the key, and which of them a script would also have flagged; review decisions; joint misses; cost per module and per error found. The comparison of S1 with S3a and with S8 is a paired bootstrap over modules, with sign flips, and leaving one translation out (\code{audit/real/TABLES.md}, \code{report.py}). Matching is by script on the declaration name and, where needed, the class; reasons are judged in the check.
\paragraph{Findings outside the key.} Decided by the human check (Section~\ref{sec:check}), with the Lean checks and HOL4 types in \code{audit/real/proofs} as evidence. Findings on study-1 modules outside the key are counted neither way.
\paragraph{Pilot with planted defects.} Ten units (\code{audit/pilot/RESULTS.md}), reported as a pilot.

\section{Human check of the reasons}\label{sec:check}
\paragraph{Who.} The author and volunteer readers from a university mathematics department, all familiar with Lean and HOL4, working on the rows in parallel, all blinded to the run, the reviewer verdict, the translation and the results. Each row had one reader, who saw only the four self-contained check files. Each reader recorded an estimate of the minutes per row. The author and the readers filled the 51-row scoring sheet together.
\paragraph{Order.} First the 51-row sheet (\code{baselines/ts\_runs\_v2/scoring}), then the 108-row sheet (\code{audit/real/check}).
\paragraph{Rows (fixed by script, seed \code{check-20261126}).} 108 rows, 91 declarations: findings of the three audit runs, chosen by rule, and some controls. The rule and the counts are in \code{audit/real/check/SPEC.md}.
\paragraph{Questions.} Is the difference real; which kind; is the stated reason right, partly, or wrong.
\paragraph{Analysis (fixed before the first answer, \code{analyse.py}).} Answers per stratum and arm; controls; known errors refuted by the check (removed from the key); recall per arm before and after; recall when the reason must also be right; the S1 against S8 difference with the module bootstrap; findings outside the key confirmed, refuted, or open. The 51-row sheet decides the provisional errors. If the control answers fail the threshold in \code{SPEC.md}, the check is reported as unreliable. Unresolved findings are counted neither way. Errors that the rule leaves out of the sheet are not checked one by one; a random sample gives an interval for them. V-C answers follow the test of Section~\ref{sec:contract}: a V-C claim whose counterexample is not checked against the relevant saved theorems stays \code{unsure}.

\section{Proving}\label{sec:proving}
Done (25 November), without HOL4 proofs, on the repaired base: 278 of 278 (Candle) and 267 of 267 (type soundness), Lean checked, statements and definitions unchanged (\code{proving\_nohol4/RESULTS.md}). Proving is one-sided evidence: proofs that fail or a statement that a proof refutes expose preservation violations that are too strong; a proof of every statement shows nothing about weak or wrong statements. Claude Opus 5.5 proves the \code{sorry} statements; definitions and statements are frozen by hash; only lemmas may be added. Measures: statements proved, transitive axiom audit, unchanged statements and definitions, statements refuted. A failed search is reported as \emph{unproved}; a statement counts as \emph{false} only with a Lean proof of its negation or a checked counterexample.

\section{Workflow loop (planned, for a later round)}\label{sec:workflow}
The full loop that version~3.3 planned, kept for a later round:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Freeze.} Pin the commits, the manifest, the briefs, the model identifiers and settings, and the budgets.
\item \textbf{Isolation.} Every model run starts in a fresh session on the PC that holds only its workspace; headless Claude Code (\code{claude -p}) in a separate directory; Codex and \code{agy} runs get only their input files. A script scans every transcript for paths outside the workspace and for web or git use.
\item \textbf{Translation} in two stages (definitions plus \code{CONVENTIONS.md}, then statements); the result must build, and a kernel check confirms that exactly the targets use \code{sorry}.
\item \textbf{Structural scans} on every revision: vacuous statements; definitions without bodies, \code{partial}, \code{opaque}, \code{axiom}, \code{native\_decide}, \code{decreasing\_by sorry}; extra or missing typeclass hypotheses; bound-variable types against HOL4's; byte identity of frozen statements.
\item \textbf{Validation round $k$ (at most 3).} Astra audits the whole translation, module by module.
\item \textbf{Evidence rule.} Every finding cites the Lean and HOL4 lines and says what would show the difference.
\item \textbf{Review} by Neon (accept or reject, class, proposed repair); no finding changes the translation without an accept.
\item \textbf{Grounding.} A script runs every mechanical check, with the query tool or \code{\#eval}/\code{decide}, 60 s limit; a contradicting check sends the finding back to Neon once; every overturn is recorded.
\item \textbf{Repair} with a minimal edit, re-checked by the scans and by Astra on the changed declarations.
\item \textbf{Stopping rule.} No finding survives review and grounding, at most 3 rounds.
\item \textbf{Proving}, one round; defects that proofs expose go back to review.
\item \textbf{Records}, packaged as an anonymous Zenodo artifact.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Decisions taken}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Framing: measured, evidence-backed audit; no certification claim (24 Nov, adopting Neon's accepted set).
\item Fidelity contract: preservation and conservativity; \code{HD []} and similar defaults are conservativity violations; baselines re-scored (author, 24 Nov). The V-C test checks counterexamples against all relevant saved theorems; unchecked cases stay unsure (author, 26 Nov). The test is met by the changed constant's introduction and a HOL4 proof that it still holds, or, for TFL definitions, a proof of each saved theorem that mentions it; the reference stays the saved theorems, not unsaved kernel definitions (author, 27 Nov). \section{Limitations, deviations and disclosures}\label{sec:limits}
\paragraph{Status of the evidence.}
\begin{itemize}
\item All model runs were finished before this version was written; the analysis of known errors was chosen after the runs and is exploratory. Only the human check was fixed in advance: its selection, questions and analysis were committed before the first answer.
\item The workflow loop of Section~\ref{sec:workflow} was not run. What ran: translation, the structural scans, one audit of every module per run, one review pass, the repair of the Candle base for proving, and proving. Scripted grounding of findings was not run, so arms S2 (Neon alone), S5, S6g, A1+G and S7 of version~3.3 have no data.
\item The planted-defect pilot is small (10 units) and reported only as a pilot.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{People.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The author designed the study and the contract and is not independent of it. Version~3.2 said that the author does not adjudicate, and version~3.3 asked for an independent person; both were given up on 25 November. The author stated on 25 November that they had not seen the results printed earlier in the work, and the documents needed for the check held no result; a progress note did tell the author that the sheet has 8 controls. The readers who shared the check did not take part in the design; each row had one reader, so no agreement between readers is measured; the split of rows between readers is not recorded in the sheet, and readers are not named. The acknowledgements thank them as a group.
\item The key, the row selection and the sheets were made by Claude, not by the author. Claude also confirmed findings outside the key before the check.
\item The minutes in the filled check sheet are each reader's estimate, not timed, so human effort for the check is approximate.
\item Readers see the claim before answering, which can anchor the answer. The sheet does not record whether any row was read twice, so agreement between readers is not measured.
\item The workflow has no human expert in the loop, but people check and score; Claude drafts briefs, labels, checks and this plan for the author's approval.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Deviations in the runs.}
\begin{itemize}
\item No auditor had the HOL4 query tool, which version~3.3 planned for every arm.
\item Astra used a JavaScript tool built into Codex (30 calls) to read and match files; one Opus call used Bash. A scan found no path outside the unit folder except tool-result files of the same session, and no network call.
\item The first 9 Neon reviews ran without the sentence restricting them to file tools. Queues and quotas delayed runs; times are in the ledger.
\item No token caps were set (author's decision for Opus and Sonnet; none for Astra and Neon).
\item Earlier proving runs in which the prover saw HOL4 proofs broke the plan's rule; they are reported as such and not as the result.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Contract choice.} Conservativity counts choices that HOL4 leaves open as violations. A different contract gives different labels; V-P and V-C are reported separately. The V-C entries made before the stricter test were rechecked (27 November): all 63 pairs hold. Seven of the pairs (\code{MAP3}, \code{type\_def\_to\_ctMap}, \code{termsem0}) depend on the reference: TFL's kernel definition, which is not saved, gives the \code{ARB} value that Lean states, so under a kernel reading they would not be V-C. The model argument rests on soundness of HOL with loose specifications; the type-soundness theories were built with \code{--fast}, so their proofs rest on CakeML's full build. The key as run and its tables are kept for comparison.
\item \textbf{Known errors} are selected: part came from scripts, the rest from named-constant tests and proofs, and they are unevenly spread over the translations. Errors outside the key show the key is incomplete; general discovery is not measured, and the totals do not describe translation errors in general.
\item \textbf{One run of Astra, two of Opus}: run-to-run variation of Astra is unknown; modules of one translation are not independent; intervals are by module and conditional on the key.
\item \textbf{Model families.} Results hold for these configurations; no claim that one family is better. The same-family and cross-family arms also differ in number of runs and cost. Agreement among agents of one family is not independent evidence.
\item \textbf{False accusations} are not reported as a rate: clean modules are few and many of their findings are undecided by the contract.
\item \textbf{Oracle limits} (Section~\ref{sec:hol4}): unresolved findings are reported as such.
\item \textbf{Proof visibility.} Translators and auditors see the HOL4 proofs; provers do not. Contamination: CakeML and Candle are public, so all models may have seen them.
\item \textbf{Model drift, CLI versions}: pinned and recorded.
\item \textbf{Two developments} in one source logic.
\end{itemize}
